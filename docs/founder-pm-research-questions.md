# Founder and product-manager research questions

These are practical questions to paste directly into Lenny's Growth Assistant. The expected answers below are concise reference answers, not strings the model must reproduce word-for-word. A correct response should preserve the same claim, guest attribution, qualification, and timestamped source.

## 1. When should a startup hire a growth leader?

**Ask:** What does Casey Winters say about when a startup should focus on growth and hire a full-time growth leader?

**Expected answer:** Do not try to scale before product-market fit. Founders should handle the early, non-scalable “kindle” strategies while designing scalable distribution into the product. Hire a specialist after a scalable “fire” strategy—such as a sales, viral, content, or paid loop—has been unlocked, so that person can make an already-working channel much stronger.

**Source:** Casey Winters, `00:50:31–00:51:51`.

## 2. Why can retention be more valuable than acquisition work?

**Ask:** Why does Dan Hockenmaier say retention matters so much in a growth model?

**Expected answer:** Growth models expose how retention compounds into referrals, content creation, contribution margin, and the size of the active customer base. Because of those interactions, a small improvement in retention can be more valuable than a larger movement elsewhere, revealing when product and growth resources are misallocated.

**Source:** Dan Hockenmaier, `00:15:45–00:16:00`.

## 3. What should be clear before hiring the first growth person?

**Ask:** What does Crystal Widjaja say a company should know before hiring its first growth person?

**Expected answer:** The company should already know where its largest growth gap is instead of hiring someone to model the entire business from scratch. The first growth hire should be statistically competent, able to design sound experiments, and focused on a small workable area with quick learning cycles rather than long tooling projects.

**Sources:** Crystal Widjaja, `00:50:58–00:51:11`, `00:52:44–00:52:49`, and `00:53:54–00:54:50`.

## 4. How should a startup find its real competitive alternatives?

**Ask:** How does April Dunford recommend approaching positioning and competitive alternatives?

**Expected answer:** Begin with what a customer would do if the product did not exist. Alternatives may include a direct competitor, a spreadsheet, manual work, an intern, or doing nothing. Then identify the capabilities that differ and repeatedly ask “so what?” to translate those differences into value competitors cannot provide.

**Sources:** April Dunford, `00:38:57–00:39:20` and `00:40:06–00:40:53`.

## 5. How can a team make weekly customer interviews sustainable?

**Ask:** How does Teresa Torres recommend making continuous customer discovery a weekly habit?

**Expected answer:** Automate recruiting so an interview appears on the team's calendar without repeated manual outreach. One approach is an in-product opt-in asking whether a customer has 20 minutes to talk, followed by scheduling software. The goal is to make weekly interviewing easier to do than to skip.

**Source:** Teresa Torres, `00:25:51–00:26:50`.

## 6. What separates an empowered product team from a feature team?

**Ask:** What does Marty Cagan say makes an empowered product team different from a feature team?

**Expected answer:** A feature team receives a roadmap of outputs—features, projects, and often dates—and is responsible for delivery. An empowered product team receives customer or business problems and is responsible for producing outcomes. The PM therefore needs deep customer, business, market, and constraint knowledge rather than merely administering a backlog.

**Sources:** Marty Cagan, `00:20:46–00:21:01` and `00:26:51–00:27:50`.

## 7. Did Airbnb eliminate product management?

**Ask:** What did Brian Chesky actually change about product management at Airbnb?

**Expected answer:** Airbnb did not simply eliminate everyone doing product work. It combined inbound product-development responsibilities with outbound product-marketing responsibilities, moved program-management work to program managers, and made the remaining product-marketing group smaller and more senior. Those leaders operate through influence in a functional organization and are expected to understand both product and distribution.

**Sources:** Brian Chesky, `00:05:04–00:05:56` and `00:06:46–00:07:42`.

## 8. When is it too early to build a growth team?

**Ask:** According to Elena Verna, when is a startup not ready for a growth team?

**Expected answer:** A startup is not ready when it lacks solid product-market fit, retention, and enough users to generate analyzable data and run experiments. With only a handful of customers, growth should remain founder-led; hiring a growth team cannot substitute for retention or a real data base.

**Source:** Elena Verna, `00:10:09–00:11:32`.

## 9. How can a startup quantify product-market fit?

**Ask:** How does Rahul Vohra measure product-market fit with the “very disappointed” survey?

**Expected answer:** Ask users how they would feel if they could no longer use the product: very disappointed, somewhat disappointed, or not disappointed. Rahul cites Sean Ellis's benchmark that struggling companies usually score below 40% “very disappointed,” while faster-growing companies commonly exceed 40%. He treats this as more predictive than NPS and uses it to focus on the users who most value the product.

**Source:** Rahul Vohra, `00:54:07–00:55:30`.

## 10. When is an execution problem actually a strategy problem?

**Ask:** What does Shreyas Doshi say about execution problems that are really strategy problems?

**Expected answer:** Teams often react to misalignment by adding reviews, meetings, and communication processes. But the underlying problem may be that teams are pursuing different strategies—or do not know the strategy—so their priorities and OKRs cannot align. The correct level of attention also depends on the situation: an early startup may genuinely need execution focus, while a platform team may need better communication as well as technical work.

**Sources:** Shreyas Doshi, `00:53:20–00:54:21` and `00:56:32–00:57:19`.

## Useful multi-turn follow-ups

After any question above, these should remain in the same research context:

- What concrete example did they give?
- What caveat should I preserve before applying that advice?
- Which exact transcript passage supports that conclusion?
- Turn that into three experiments my team could run, separating the guest's claims from your suggestions.
- Compare that view with another guest who disagrees or adds an important qualification.
